# OECD AI Principles (2019, updated 2024)

> **Status:** Active — adopted by 46 countries; foundational baseline for most national AI frameworks
> **Binding:** NO — recommendations (soft law), but influential in shaping binding legislation
> **Confidence:** HIGH — published by OECD, widely cited
> **Source:** <https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles>

## Five Principles for Responsible Stewardship of Trustworthy AI

### 1. Inclusive Growth, Sustainable Development, and Well-Being

AI should benefit people and the planet by driving inclusive growth, sustainable development, and well-being.

**Technical implications:**

- Consider societal impact beyond direct users
- Assess environmental impact of AI training/inference
- Design for accessibility and inclusiveness

### 2. Human-Centred Values and Fairness

AI actors should respect the rule of law, human rights, democratic values, and diversity. AI systems should include safeguards to enable fairness.

**Technical implications:**

- Fairness testing across demographic groups
- Anti-discrimination safeguards
- Respect for privacy and data protection
- Mechanisms for user agency (opt-out, override)

### 3. Transparency and Explainability

AI actors should commit to transparency and responsible disclosure regarding AI systems. People should be enabled to understand AI outcomes and challenge them.

**Technical implications:**

- Disclose AI usage to affected persons
- Provide understandable explanations of AI decisions
- Document AI system capabilities and limitations
- Enable meaningful human review of AI outputs

### 4. Robustness, Security, and Safety

AI systems should be robust, secure, and safe throughout their lifecycle. Risks should be continuously assessed and managed.

**Technical implications:**

- Adversarial testing and red-teaming
- Continuous monitoring for performance degradation
- Fail-safe mechanisms and graceful degradation
- Security controls against manipulation and misuse

### 5. Accountability

AI actors should be accountable for the proper functioning of AI systems, in line with the above principles.

**Technical implications:**

- Clear governance structures and roles
- Audit trails for AI decisions
- Incident response procedures
- Mechanisms for redress when AI causes harm

## Five Recommendations for Governments

1. **Investing in AI R&D** — public and private investment in trustworthy AI
2. **Fostering a digital ecosystem** — accessible data, computing, digital infrastructure
3. **Shaping an enabling policy environment** — regulatory frameworks, standards
4. **Building human capacity** — education, training, workforce transition
5. **International co-operation** — sharing best practices, interoperability of frameworks

## OECD AI Principles as a Mapping Baseline

Most national AI frameworks explicitly reference or build upon the OECD Principles:

| Jurisdiction | Alignment |
| ------------- | ----------- |
| EU AI Act | Art. 9 (safety), Art. 10 (fairness), Art. 13 (transparency), Art. 14 (oversight) — all map to OECD principles |
| Singapore AIGF | Four key areas directly parallel OECD principles |
| US NIST AI RMF | Trustworthy AI characteristics align with OECD principles |
| UK Pro-Innovation | Five UK principles directly derived from OECD |
| Japan | Social Principles explicitly cite OECD |
| Australia | Eight AI Ethics Principles expand on OECD |
| Canada AIDA | Built on OECD framework |
| ASEAN Guide | Directly references OECD principles |
