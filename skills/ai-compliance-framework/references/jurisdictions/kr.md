# South Korea — AI Basic Act

> **Status:** Enforced — AI Basic Act effective January 22, 2026 (penalty provisions phased to January 2027)
> **Binding:** YES — mandatory for high-impact AI operators; PIPA remains binding for data protection
> **Confidence:** HIGH — enacted law (Act No. 20676, enacted Jan 21, 2025; Enforcement Decree effective Jan 22, 2026)
> **Sources:**

> - Framework Act on AI Development and Trust (AI Basic Act, Act No. 20676)
> - Enforcement Decree of the AI Basic Act (effective Jan 22, 2026)

## AI Basic Act (Framework Act on AI Development and Trust)

> **Enacted:** January 21, 2025 | **Effective:** January 22, 2026
> **Significance:** Described as the world's first enforceable mandatory regime for high-impact AI operators

### Key Provisions

- **High-Impact AI classification:** Government defines high-impact AI systems requiring enhanced obligations
- **Impact assessments:** Mandatory for high-impact AI systems before deployment
- **Transparency obligations:** AI service providers must disclose AI use to users
- **Safety standards and certification:** National AI safety standards established
- **National AI Committee:** Established under the Prime Minister's Office for policy coordination
- **AI Ethics Committee:** Advisory body for ethical AI governance

### High-Impact AI Requirements

Operators of high-impact AI must:

1. Conduct and publish AI impact assessments
2. Implement human oversight mechanisms
3. Maintain transparency about AI decision-making processes
4. Report AI safety incidents to authorities
5. Comply with technical safety standards

### Penalty Provisions (Effective January 2027)

> **Note:** Penalty provisions are postponed one year from the Act's effective date to allow compliance preparation.

- Administrative penalties for non-compliance with high-impact AI obligations
- Corrective orders from regulatory authorities
- Specific penalty amounts to be defined in Enforcement Decree

### Technical Directives

1. Classify AI systems against the high-impact AI criteria published by the National AI Committee
2. Implement impact assessment procedures before high-impact AI deployment
3. Build user-facing disclosure mechanisms for AI involvement in decisions
4. Establish incident reporting pipelines to the relevant authorities
5. Implement human review gates for high-impact AI decisions
6. Maintain audit logs sufficient for regulatory inspection

## Data Protection: PIPA

- Comprehensive personal information protection law (effective 2020, amended 2023)
- Consent required for collection, use, and provision of personal information
- Sensitive information (health, biometrics, race, ideology, political views) — explicit consent + restrictions
- Cross-border transfer: Consent or equivalent protection level
- PIPC (Personal Information Protection Commission) is supervisory authority
- Mandatory breach notification
- Automated decision-making provisions (2023 amendment) — right to explanation and refusal
